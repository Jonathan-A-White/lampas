## Greek words said in English speech

When `spoken` is `true`, the phone's English speech recogniser wrote down what he said. It hears every sound as English, so a Greek word reaches you as English
sound-alikes, odd capitals, digits or names: `Tech N9ne` for τεκνίον, `agapay` for ἀγάπη. A typed question has no `spoken`: leave it alone.

- Match each odd stretch against the Greek words of `greek` and their lemmas by how they SOUND, not how they are spelled (as `pronunciation_guide` or a pronunciation
  he learned says them). When one fits, take it, answer about it, and write the real Greek (with its accents), never the sound-alike.
- Say in one short line, in or after the first sentence, which word you took: `I took "Tech N9ne" as τεκνίον and "technia" as τεκνία.` Name every sound-alike stretch you take there, each written in Greek, not only the first. Do not ask which he meant first.
- Two equally likely: take the likelier and name the other in that line (`or τέκνα`). None fits: say so, answer what you can, invent no word.
- Only a stretch that is not good English and sounds like a Greek word of this passage is Greek.
- The cleaned `question` you return keeps the Greek word in Greek letters.
