# Byzantine, Nestle-Aland and ECM

Each other reading Lampas shows carries a small sign that says whose reading it is: **B**, **NA** or **ECM**. Each names a different source.

## The Byzantine text, and B

The Greek you read is the Byzantine Textform as Maurice Robinson and William Pierpont edited it (2005; their 2018 edition changes a few accents and a very few readings). It follows the reading most Byzantine manuscripts share. The editors built it chiefly from the large apparatus of Hermann von Soden (1911), and in Revelation from the collations of Herman Hoskier (1929).

Most of the time the Byzantine manuscripts agree. Where they are closely divided, the editors print one reading in the text and the other beside it, in the margin of the 2005 edition and in an upper apparatus in 2018. Those other readings are marked **B** in Lampas: "the Byzantine text is split here". They are few in most books and many in Revelation (about 350 of its places), where the Byzantine manuscripts fall into two streams of nearly equal support.

## Nestle-Aland, and NA

*Novum Testamentum Graece*, edited by Barbara and Kurt Aland and others, is the text most modern Bibles are translated from. Its 27th edition came out in 1993 and its 28th in 2012. Its editors choose each reading place by place, giving the most weight to the oldest manuscripts (among them the great fourth-century copies Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus) and asking which reading explains the rest.

The 2018 edition's lower apparatus lists the places where the Nestle-Aland text differs from the Byzantine text (all but some small matters of spelling), not only the ones Nestle-Aland's own apparatus notes. Those readings are marked **NA**. The 28th edition changed the text of the letters from James to Jude, so there the apparatus sometimes names NA27 or NA28 alone; the sheet spells it out. Where Nestle-Aland puts words in square brackets, its editors were unsure of them, and the brackets are shown as they are printed.

## The Editio Critica Maior, and ECM

The *Editio Critica Maior* is a very large edition, prepared in Münster and published book by book, with the evidence of every manuscript it uses. The Robinson-Pierpont 2018 apparatus gives its readings only for Acts (the ECM of Acts appeared in 2017). So in Lampas, **ECM readings are in Acts only, at 18 places**. At twelve of them Nestle-Aland also differs from the Byzantine text, and the two critical editions differ from each other: in Acts 18:7 the man's name is Justus in the Byzantine text, Titius Justus in Nestle-Aland and Titus Justus in the ECM. At the other six only the ECM differs from the Byzantine text.

## What the signs do not say

A sign says which edition prints a reading. It does not say how many manuscripts have it, how old they are, or which reading is right. Robinson and Pierpont hold their main text to be the better one; the Nestle-Aland and ECM editors hold theirs to be. The Textus Receptus behind the King James Version is a third text: the 2005 preface counts over 1,800 places where it differs from the Byzantine Textform.

Further reading, named and not quoted: Zane Hodges and Arthur Farstad's *The Greek New Testament according to the Majority Text*, another edition of the majority text; Tommy Wasserman and Peter Gurry's *A New Approach to Textual Criticism*, on the method the ECM editors use.
