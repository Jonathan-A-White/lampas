# Grinds: examples and instruction parts

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md (mw-gq6.485). CLAUDE.md keeps the rule every grind story follows: change a grind's behaviour, update or add its examples in the same story.

Each grind (`grinds/<kind>.json`) keeps BDD-style scenarios under `grinds/examples/<kind>/<name>.json`: the request (with `schemaVersion`), the recording beside it where the grind takes one (verse-read), and an `expect` block of simple checks on answer fields. Format: `grinds/examples/README.md`. `mw grist smoke lampas` runs them against the live grist; `tests/unit/grind-examples.test.ts` validates every request against `grinds/<kind>.input.schema.json` and every `expect` path against the answer schema, and fails when a grind has no scenario or no input schema.

The bible-talk instructions are one part per section (mw-skwocd.8): `grinds/bible-talk.instructions/` holds `NNN-<section>.md`, the first the `#` title and each other one `##` section (its `###` subsections with it), numbered in tens so name order is the order the model reads them; `grinds/<kind>.json`'s `instructions` names the directory, and the mill reads its `*.md` parts in name order, joined by one blank line. **A story edits only the part of the section it changes.** A new section is a new part numbered between its neighbours (`255-<section>.md`), never a renumbering; each part ends in one newline. Tests read a grind's instructions whole through `scripts/grind-parts.ts` `instructionsOf(kind)`, never by path (`tests/unit/grind-parts.test.ts` fails on a test that reads one by path, and holds the split to the file it came from, byte for byte). The other grinds' instructions are still one file each.

Every grind's instructions stay under 100,000 bytes, as `cat grinds/<kind>.instructions/*.md | wc -c` counts them (mw-5r3p30.728): every ask pays for all of them, and the mill warns past that. `tests/unit/grind-instructions-size.test.ts` fails at 100,000; make room by saying a rule once, not by dropping one (`docs/design/bible-talk-trim-map.md` is how the bible-talk trim did it).

**Bible talk, an Old Testament verse (mw-gdcyf98.18).** The request may carry `hebrew` (the verses' pointed Hebrew, cantillation off) and `hebrew_words` in the place of `greek` and `greek_words`, and `held_passages[]` an Old Testament passage's `hebrew` in the place of its `greek` (the schema's `anyOf`); the answer's `words` entries may carry `language` ('he' for a Hebrew word). The instruction part that teaches the tutor this is `245-a-verse-of-the-old-testament.md` (mw-gdcyf98.29: what `hebrew` and `hebrew_words` are, quote no Greek, a Hebrew word in `words` with `language` `he` and its pointed form in the field named `greek`, `settings.hebrewDepth`, Modern Israeli when a word is said), with `030`'s sentence on `language`, `240` no longer saying Hebrew never goes in `words`, and `200`'s word link taking a Hebrew lemma on an Old Testament chapter. Its examples are `hebrew-verse-talk` (Jonah 1:1, a word link too), `hebrew-chapter-talk` (Genesis 1) and `hebrew-offer-on-genesis` (Hebrew 0: the English alone).

**Bible talk, maps (mw-zbjgn3r.4).** The request may carry `maps` (the maps of the passage's chapter, `{id, source, title, period, places}`, at most 12, Bible Mapper's first and at most two Logos rows last, titled '(opens in Logos)'; docs/maps.md 'The tutor brings up maps') and the answer's `links` may carry `{kind: 'map', map: <id>}`, an id of a line of `maps`. The rule is the `### Links to a map` paragraph of part 200, and the examples are `map-of-the-passage`, `map-in-logos` and `no-map-listed`; verse-ask has neither the field nor the link.

## copywork (mw-5r3p30.899, dictation mw-5r3p30.900)

`copywork` judges a photographed page of copywork against the verse (the Verse view's Practise > Copywork, docs/verse-view.md 'Practise'). The request is `{reference, language: 'en'|'grc'|'hbo', words}` with one picture (up to 1 MB, jpeg, png or webp); the answer marks each word `right`, `missing` or `wrong` (with a `problem` naming the letter, accent, breathing or vowel point), says whether the page was `readable`, and gives `fix_first`. Its examples are `grinds/examples/copywork/`: a perfect copy and one with errors in each language, John 1:1 in English and Greek, Genesis 1:1 in Hebrew.

**Dictation (mw-5r3p30.900)** is the same grind: the request may carry `mode: 'dictation'` (left out for copywork), meaning the app read `words` aloud a phrase at a time with them hidden and he wrote what he heard, and the answer's wrong words may carry `sounds_alike: true` (what he wrote sounds like the model word, spelled as another word or another way; never on a `right` or `missing` word or in copywork). The instructions' `## Dictation` says how. Its examples are `grinds/examples/copywork/*-dictation-*`: Genesis 1:3 in English with `sed` and `their`, John 1:5 in Greek with `σκοτεία` and `φένει`, Genesis 1:1 in Hebrew with `ע` for `א` and a segol for a tsere, each with a perfect page beside it (John 1:1 English and Greek, Genesis 1:3 Hebrew). The photos are rendered, not handwritten, by `scripts/render-copywork-examples.ts` (mw-5r3p30.952): the repo's Literata, Gentium Plus and Noto Serif Hebrew on ruled paper, each word nudged off the line by a fixed pseudo-random amount, 1600 px wide (`TALK_MAX_EDGE`, the long edge the phone sends) with the type large enough that a vowel point is a blob of 8 px or more, as on a page photographed to fill the frame (the first pages were 900 px with 4 px points and the model honestly misread them: four `expect` lines were dropped in a day). The words of a page are its example's `request.words`; a page that holds a mistake says which words are replaced or left out in the script's `COPYWORK_PAGES` (`sed`, `begining`, `ἀρχή`, a Hebrew word without points), so a new example gets a line there (`tests/unit/copywork-example-pages.test.ts` fails without one). Re-render with `npx tsx scripts/render-copywork-examples.ts` (it needs Playwright's Chromium) and commit the pages: a second run, or `--check`, changes no byte. A change of the instructions is proved with `mw grist eval lampas copywork --example <name> --ref <branch>`, and a real page of handwriting is the Governor's to try (how often the grind calls a real handwritten page wrong is not measured until he photographs one).

## narration (mw-5r3p30.901)

`narration` answers a retelling (the Verse view's Practise > Narration, docs/verse-view.md 'Practise'). The request is `{reference, passage, narration}`: the passage in English and what he told back, written down by the app from the phone's English dictation (a capital to begin, a full stop to end, nothing else). No picture. The answer is `{caught[], missed[], in_order, question}`: the passage's main points he told and those he left out (each a short phrase, both in the passage's order, every point in exactly one list), whether he told them in that order, and one gentle question that leads to the first missed point without saying it (deeper into the passage when nothing was missed). The instructions say a point told in other words is caught (narration is in his own words), that a misheard name is read for what he means, and never to grade him in a field. Its examples are `grinds/examples/narration/`: John 1:1 missing its last point, Genesis 1:3-5 told in full, Mark 4:37-41 told out of order (`in_order` false), John 3:16 told as a story about bread (nothing caught) and Luke 15:11-24 missing its middle. A change of the instructions is proved with `mw grist eval lampas narration --example <name> --ref <branch>`.

## summary (mw-5r3p30.902)

`summary` judges a summary (the Verse view's Practise > Summary, docs/verse-view.md 'Practise'). The request is `{reference, passage, how: 'typed'|'photo', summary?}`: the verse, passage or chapter in English (up to 20,000 characters, a whole chapter) and either what he typed (`how: 'typed'`) or one picture of his handwriting (`how: 'photo'`, up to 1 MB, jpeg, png or webp, and no `summary`). The answer is `{readable, read_as, present[], missing[], in_order, length, model_summary, advice}`: the passage's main points he gave and those he left out (each a short phrase, in the passage's order, every point in exactly one list; a point told in other words is present), whether he gave them in that order, whether the length is `right`, `too_long` (it retells the passage with its details: the usual fault of a summary) or `too_short` (too thin to carry a point), one sentence of a model summary and one piece of advice. For a photo `read_as` is the page as the tutor read it (the app puts it in the box so he can correct it); an unreadable page is `readable: false` with `present` and `missing` empty, `model_summary` empty and the advice saying how to photograph it. Its examples are `grinds/examples/summary/`: John 1:1 with two of three points, Mark 4:35-41 told out of order (`in_order` false), Genesis 1:1-5 retold line by line (`too_long`), Luke 15:11-24 as "A son." (`too_short`), the whole of Genesis 1 in two sentences, a rendered handwritten page of John 1:1-5 and a blank page. A change of the instructions is proved with `mw grist eval lampas summary --example <name> --ref <branch>`.

## drill-items: a Hebrew request (mw-gdcyf98.21)

`drill-items` is a background grind: after a Review round it writes short questions on the ideas he missed, from verses he reads. A request is of one language. A Hebrew one has `language: 'he'`, Hebrew ladder ideas (ids start `he-`) and verses with `hebrew` and `hebrew_words` in place of `greek` (the input schema's `anyOf`); the instructions' `## A Hebrew request` section says the words are copied with their points, a word with a maqaf or an inseparable prefix is one word, and the parsing is written in plain words. Its examples are `grinds/examples/drill-items/hebrew-article.json` (`he-article`, Genesis 1:1-5) and `hebrew-qal-perfect.json` (`he-perfect`, Jonah 1). The quiz on a Hebrew verse in the Talk sheet is the `bible-talk` grind in quiz mode on an Old Testament verse (the request carries `hebrew`); `grinds/bible-talk.instructions/170-quiz-mode.md` does not name `hebrew` yet because the instructions have no room (docs/hebrew.md 'Quiz me on a Hebrew verse').
