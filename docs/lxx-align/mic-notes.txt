Micah 1 to 7 are done (story mw-u2w5klk.349.66: 807 runs, `data/lxx/align/mic-01` to `mic-07`, `mic.expect.json`): 371 of the 1,238 English content words sat where a tap opened no Greek word before the pass (58, 43, 41, 55, 56, 44 and 74 in the seven chapters), and 0 do after.
These are all seven chapters of Micah the ship ships, so no chapter of the book is left for another batch.
The pass wrote 322 fix rows (31, 47, 39, 45, 44, 44 and 72) for the aligner's own pairs that left a content word with no Greek or gave it another's: 'like' and 'wax' of 1:4 sat on κηρὸς and προσώπου (ὡς κηρὸς is now the one chunk 'like wax'), 'mourning' of 1:8 sat on κόψεται (it is now on πένθος), 'weights' and 'deceitful' of 6:11 were crossed with 'the wicked', 'prepare' and 'mischief' of 7:3 were swapped, and 'many' and 'nations' of 4:2 were crossed with 'go'; a swap of two Greek words between neighbours is one fix row for each chunk in the one verse.
Verbs with a particle or an auxiliary ('shall be shaken', 'will rise up', 'shall utterly destroy', 'have been utterly destroyed') and a modifier of a noun ('graven images', 'fat goats', 'first-ripe fruit') are each one chunk on the Greek word that renders them; a fix row names the chunk as the verse reads after the no-Greek words in front of it are joined to it ('I will utterly destroy your sorceries', not 'utterly destroy your sorceries').
The 8 English content words settled as rendering no Greek word are in the sidecar, each with its reason, and are all of one kind: Brenton's English stands in the verse next to the one its Greek is in. 'assembly' and 'Lord' of 2:6 ('in the assembly of the Lord') render ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ Κυρίου, the end of Swete's 2:5; 'breach' and 'made' of 2:12 ('through the breach made before them:') render the start of Swete's 2:13; 'away', 'goods', 'take' and 'therefore' of 7:4 ('therefore I will take away their goods') render καὶ ἐξελοῦμαι τὰ ἀγαθὰ αὐτῶν, the end of Swete's 7:3.
A model chunk cannot point out of its verse, so these three are for LXX2012_SPLITS (scripts/data/tvtms.ts; a head or a tail of a verse stood at the next or the previous Greek verse, as for Jonah 2:7 and Hosea 6:3), which this story did not touch: 2:12 ends with a tail for the Greek 2:13, and 7:4 begins with a head for the Greek 7:3; 2:6's words stand in the middle of the verse, which a head or a tail does not name. The Greek words they would render (Greek 2:5 words 9 to 11, 2:13 words 0 to 4, 7:3 words 20 to 24) have no English in the ship, and Micah 7:3's 'desire' keeps the aligner's pairs with 22 to 24 (τὰ ἀγαθὰ αὐτῶν) because a fix may not leave a Greek word that had English with none.
